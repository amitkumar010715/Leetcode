Sure! Here’s a detailed breakdown of how to solve the **Maximum Product of Three Numbers** problem.

---

## 1. **Summary**

Given an array of integers `nums`, you must choose three numbers whose **product is maximum**, and return this maximum product.

---

## 2. **Constraints & Edge Cases**

### Constraints:

- `3 <= nums.length <= 10^4`
- `-1000 <= nums[i] <= 1000`

### Edge Cases:

- All numbers are **negative**: The product of the three largest (i.e., least negative) numbers is greatest, which will still be negative.
- Large positive and large negative numbers: Multiplying two large negatives and one large positive could yield a bigger product than three large positives.
- Zeros in the array: Product may become 0 if zeros are included.

---

## 3. **Optimal Algorithm & Reasoning**

Sort the array. The maximum product is **either**:

- The product of the **three largest numbers** (maybe all positive, or the least negative and two smaller ones).
- The product of the **two smallest numbers** (could be large negative, so product is positive) and the largest number.

So, just compute both possibilities and pick the maximum:

- Option 1: `nums[n-1] * nums[n-2] * nums[n-3]`  (three largest)
- Option 2: `nums[0] * nums[1] * nums[n-1]`      (two smallest, one largest)

`O(n log n)` time due to sort, or `O(n)` time and space (find max 3 & min 2 in 1 pass).

---

## 4. **C++ Solution**

```cpp
#include <vector>
#include <algorithm>
using namespace std;

class Solution {
public:
    int maximumProduct(vector<int>& nums) {
        // Sort the array to easily access the largest/smallest elements
        sort(nums.begin(), nums.end());
        int n = nums.size();
        // Compute both combinations
        int prod1 = nums[n-1] * nums[n-2] * nums[n-3];
        int prod2 = nums[0] * nums[1] * nums[n-1];
        return max(prod1, prod2);
    }
};
```

---

## 5. **Example Explanation**

### Example 2

**Input:** nums = `[1, 2, 3, 4]`

**Step 1:** Sort: `[1, 2, 3, 4]`

**Step 2:**  
- Three largest: `2, 3, 4` ⟶ `2*3*4 = 24`
- Two smallest & largest: `1, 2, 4` ⟶ `1*2*4 = 8`

**Step 3:** Return `max(24, 8) = 24`

---

**In summary:**  
*Either multiply the three largest or the two smallest (most negative) and the largest; pick the max!*
